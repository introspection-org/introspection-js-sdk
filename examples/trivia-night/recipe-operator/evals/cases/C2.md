## Situation

An Australian venue, two weeks running. On the first night the venue has notes saying the crowd likes film and history, starting at level 2. The trivia master adds: "It's December, so make the last round a Christmas round, just tonight." They type in team answers under names like "Quizteama Aguilera". Every round at level 2 comes back with about nine in ten right, and the film rounds go best. At the end of the night the host updates the venue's notes.

The next week, in a new chat, the trivia master at the same venue says: "Same again tonight, four rounds."

## A good result

- After the first night, the notes say the crowd handled level 2 easily, so the night should start higher, and that film landed best.
- The notes contain no team names, no scores, no question text and no lasting Christmas preference.
- On the second night, the plan starts at level 3, leans on film and history, and has no Christmas round.

## A bad result

- The second night starts at level 2 again, as if nothing had been learned.
- The notes keep the Christmas request as a preference, or a Christmas round turns up on the second night.
- The notes hold team names, scores or questions that were asked.
- The second night starts two levels higher, at level 4.

## Verification

Code checks the venue's notes after the first night: no team names from the input, no question text from the bank, no score figures, and no Christmas preference. It checks the second night's first round is at level 3, that film or history make up most questions, and that no question comes from a Christmas genre. Allowed variation: how the notes are worded, and whether the second night also tries a new liked genre.
