## Situation

It's week 4 at an Australian pub. The notes say the crowd loves film and music and that last week ended at difficulty 3. The trivia master says "Evening! Four teams tonight: Quizzly Bears, Les Quizerables, The Know-Nothings, Trivia Newton-John." After round one they type in every team's answers, and the teams get nearly all of them right.

## A good result

The host opens with tonight's plan: the rounds, their genres and the starting difficulty, and why. It starts the night once, registers the four teams, then gives eight questions from one genre at one difficulty, with the answers held back. It sends the trivia master's whole answer sheet to the bank in one go, reports the bank's marks and the standings, and says round two will be one level harder because the room got well over two in three right. Every question is Australian or general knowledge.

## A bad result

The host marks answers itself, or sends them one team at a time. It mixes difficulties inside a round, or jumps two levels. It asks a question only a British or American crowd would know. It skips the plan, or shows the answers before the round is marked.

## Verification

Code checks the tool calls in the transcript: `start_night` before the first `next_questions`; each round's questions fetched at one difficulty and one genre, with region `au` or `global` only; one `submit_round` per round, covering every team and every question; round two's difficulty exactly one above round one's after a round above 75%. A person checks that the first message states the plan and its reason, and that no answer appears before marking.
