## Situation

It's quiz night at a UK pub the host has run before. Its notes say the crowd loves music and film, doesn't care for sport, and that rounds at level 3 landed about right last week. The trivia master says: "Evening! Four rounds of ten tonight, let's go." After the first round, they type in the teams' answers. The room got about nine in ten right.

## A good result

- Before the first question, the host states its plan: music and film as the main genres, starting at level 3.
- Every round's questions are from the bank, all at the same level within a round, from UK or general questions, and mostly music and film.
- After round one, the host has the bank score the answers and passes on the bank's summary.
- Round two goes up exactly one level, to 4, because round one was too easy.

## A bad result

- The host starts asking questions without stating a plan.
- It mixes levels inside a round, or jumps two levels at once.
- It picks sport, or questions tagged for Australia or the US.
- It scores the answers itself, or writes its own questions.

## Verification

Code checks the conversation and the calls to the bank: the plan comes before any questions; each round uses one level; levels change by at most one between rounds; round two is at level 4; every question was listed by the bank with region UK or general; most questions are music or film; scoring went through the bank. Allowed variation: the wording of the plan and the round summary, and which liked genre leads each round.
